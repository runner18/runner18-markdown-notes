## The CRA; Annexes I-VII
### Annex I: Essential Cybersecurity Requirements

#### Part I Cybersecurity requirements related to the properties of products with digital elements
Products must:
- Have appropriate cybersecurity based on risk
- Not have known vulnerabilities
- In case of incident, minimize damage
- Have proper reporting
- Security updates
- Protect confidentiality and integrity of data
- Protect essential functionality
- Allow users to completely wipe data
#### Part II Vulnerability handling requirements
Manufacturers of products must:
- Create software bill of materials
- Monitor for vulnerabilities in any item in software BOM
- Make security updates available without delay
- Disclose reasons for security updates unless it's too risky

### Annex II: Information and Instructions to the User
Product must include
- Uniquely identifying info about product
- Contact info for receiving/sending vulnerability info
- How to install security updates
- How to safely decommission product
- How to safely use product and its cybersecurity risks
- How to disable automatic security updates
- End date of security support

## The CRA; Annexes I-VIII

### Annex I: Essential Cybersecurity Requirements

**Part I Cybersecurity requirements related to the properties of products with digital elements**
1. Digital products' must ensure appropriate cybersecurity based on risk
2. According to Article 13(2), digital products must
	a. Not have any known exploits
	b. Have a secure default configuration
	c. Have security updates and auto-updates with opt-out option
	d. Have authentication and authentication reporting
	e. Protect confidentiality via state of the art encryption
	f. Protect data integrity from manipulation, and report data corruption
	g. Don't pass in extra personal data if you don't have to (data minimization)
	h. Protect essential functions against denial-of-service attacks
	i. Minimize your device messing with other devices on the network
	j. Limit attack services, including external interfaces
	k. Reduce impact of incidents
	l. Log security-related information, include opt-out for user
	m. Include option for users to wipe all data entirely

**Part II Vulnerability handling requirements**
Manufacturers of digital products shall:
1. Draw up software bill of materials, and document vulnerabilities
2. Don't delay on fixing vulnerabilities and providing security updates
3. Apply regular tests/review of the digital product's security
4. Communicate how you fixed vulnerability (can delay fix if safer to keep secret)
5. Create a coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy
6. Have contact information so others can report vulnerabilities to you
7. Have way to safety push out security updates
8. Security updates should be without delay, free, and informative

### Annex II: Information and Instructions to the User
At minimum, digital product should accompanied by:
1. Manufacturer name, contact info, website
2. Point of contact for reporting vulnerabilities
3. Name, type, other unique id info for digital product
4. Purpose of product, security properties from manufacturer
5. Possible foreseeable misuse based on its intended purpose
6. URL link to EU declaration of conformity website
7. The end-date of the security support period
8. Detailed instructions on
	a. How to use product to ensure its secure use
	b. How product changes can affect cybersecurity
	c. How to install security updates
	d. How to securely decommission the product
	e. How to turn off automatic security updates (See Annex I, Part I, point 2c, )
	f. If product integrates with others, compliance information for integration
9. If software bill of materials available to user, info on where to access it

### Annex VIII

#### Part I Conformity assessment procedure based on internal control (based on module A)
1. Internal Control - the procedure where manufacture fulfils: points 2, 3, and 4 of this part, and Parts I and II of Annex I
2. Manufacturer shall draw up technical documentation described in Annex VII.
3. Design, development, production and vulnerability handling of digital products:
	1. Manufacturer shall ensure compliance with Parts I and II of Annex I
4. Conformity marking and declaration of conformity
	1. Manufacturer shall affix the CE marking to each digital product
	2. Manufacturer shall draw up EU declaration of conformity for each product in accordance with Article 28, and maintain for 10 years after product is placed on market
5. Authorized representatives
	1. The manufacturer's obligations on point 4 may be fulfilled by its authorized representative

#### Part II EU-type examination (based on module B)
1. EU-type examination - the part of a conformity assessment procedure where notified body examines digital product and vulnerability handling processes, and attests that the digital product meets Parts I and II of Annex I
2. EU-type examination is carried out by looking at looking at technical documentation and supporting evidence (see point 3), and examination of one or more critical parts of product
3. The manufacturer shall lodge an application for EU-type examination with a single notified body of its choice. The application shall include
	1. Name/address of manufacturer or authorized representative
	2. Written declaration that same application for examination wasn't submitted elsewhere
	3. Technical documentation for assessing the conformity according to both Parts I and II of Annex I. Documentation shall specify design, manufacture, and product operation, and contain elements set out in Annex VII
	4. Supporting evidence of security - mention any used documents, particularly where relevant harmonized standards or technical specs have not been applied in full. Evidence should include test results carried out by the appropriate testing lab, if necessary.
4. The notified body shall:
	1. Examine technical documentation and supporting evidence according to Parts I and II of Annex I
	2. Verify the product was manufactured according to technical documentation, and identify which elements in accordance with the relevant harmonized standards or technical specs
	3. Carry out the appropriate tests to examine solutions are applied to relevant harmonized standards or technical standards for Annex I requirements.
	4. Carry out appropriate tests to examine solutions that are NOT applied to relevant harmonized standards or technical standards for Annex I requirements
	5. Agree with the manufacturer on a location where tests will be carried out
5. The notified body shall draw up and release an evaluation report that records the activities taken in point 4
6. Where the vulnerability handling processes meet the cybersecurity requirements in Annex I, the notified body shall issue an EU-type examination certificate to the manufacturer. The certificate shall contain the name/address of manufacturer, examination conclusions, any conditions for its validity, and the necessary identification data for vulnerability handling processes. The certificate may have one or more annexes attached.
	1. The certificate shall contain all relevant information for product conformity, with vulnerability handling processes to be evaluated and to allow for in-service control.
	2. The notify body shall refuse to issue an EU-type examination certificate where requirements are not met
7. The notified body must be kept in the know of any changes that indicate the vulnerability handling process may no longer apply
