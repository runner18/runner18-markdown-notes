Say you have
- diff eq $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=y$

And say you know one of the diff eq's solutions has coordinate point (0,1) or $y(0)=1$
What does this equation look like?

With a fancy computer, we could run a simulator that could:
- start at (0,1)
- as x changes, almost continuously update $y$ based on $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ (aka $\dfrac{change\ in\ y}{change\ in\ x}$)
- and almost continuously update $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ to equal y, as per the diff eq

But we're not a fancy computer. 
HOWEVER we can get a rough estimate by only updating $y$ and $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ every time x increases by 1.

For each step (x=0, x=1, x=2, etc.) we:
- Increase y by $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
- Update $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ to be equal to $y$

Let's start with the coordinates we were given, x=0 and y=1

| x<br>x = (last x) + 1 | y<br>y = (last y) + (last $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$)<br> | $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$<br>$\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ = (this y), according to diff eq |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0                     | **1**                                           | 1                                                                     |
| 1                     | **2** = 1 + 1                                   | 2                                                                     |
| 2                     | **4** = 2 + 2                                   | 4                                                                     |
| 3                     | **8** = 4 + 4                                   | 8                                                                     |
Basically, we're hand crafting crafting a rough estimate of what the equation should look like.
